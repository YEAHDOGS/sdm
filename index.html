Dating apps turned people into profiles. We turned the volume back up.

Somewhere between the fifth reswipe and the third dead chat, the whole thing started feeling like inventory management. SDM starts from a different question: what if the app knew your taste before it showed you a face? Your top artists, your late-night rotations, the things you're into and the things you're not — matched first, small talk second.

Music

Your top artists do the talking. Connect Spotify or Apple Music and SDM reads your actual taste — not the three bands you listed to look interesting in 2019.

Shared obsessions are the strongest signal we have. Two people with the same 2 a.m. rotation already have a first date.

Chemistry

Answer once, match deep. Optional assessments map the stuff that actually decides compatibility — appetites, boundaries, vices, the things polite apps won't ask about.

Everything is opt-in. Your answers are encrypted, never shown on your profile, and only ever used to compute fit. Skip anything. Delete everything.

Humans

Every account backed by a real ID. No bots, no catfish farms, no "models" messaging you at scale. AI patrols for spam around the clock; humans handle the appeals.

When everyone in the room is verified, the room gets honest fast.

The jungle has rules.

Wild doesn't mean lawless. A few non-negotiables keep the animals safe.

  • 18+, verified, no exceptions

    Government ID check before you can talk to anyone. US only at launch — one country's rules, done right, before we go anywhere else.

  • Your business stays your business

    Preferences are compatibility signals, encrypted and minimized. We don't warehouse IDs and we don't sell data — there's nothing to sell.

  • Signals, not transactions

    What you're into is a matching input. Buying or selling anything — especially drugs — is an instant, permanent ban. The jungle provides; the app doesn't deal.

  • Talk first

    Random text, voice, and video chat plus chatrooms — conversation is the product, not the prelude. If you can't hold a chat, the algorithm noticed.
